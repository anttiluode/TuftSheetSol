# TuftSheetSol — The World and Its Medium

**[Offline interactive visual laboratory](index.html)** · [Model](model.js) · [Development and held-seed protocol](PROTOCOL.md)

TuftSheetSol tests a question that emerged from the discussions around TuftSheet, WaveCortex and SyntheticQualia:

> Can a distributed predictive world-model remain internally coherent while a physical disturbance temporarily compromises the way local columns read it?

The design deliberately keeps the **world state** distinct from the **state of the physical medium**. It is not an assertion that migraine aura is generated by this mechanism, and not a claim about artificial subjective experience.

## Open the lab

Open **index.html directly in any desktop browser**. No internet, server, GPU, libraries or installation required. The page contains four aligned maps:

1. **Outside world** — two opposite hidden sign rules (shown to the experimenter, not to the simulated columns).
2. **Internal context** — local context estimates propagated sideways, with a pre-supplied weak boundary link.
3. **Perceptual readout** — the local context after a hand-specified physiological modulation.
4. **Excitability / recovery** — an independent activator/refractory reaction–diffusion sheet. Click anywhere to seed a travelling disturbance.

The model runs an initial learning warm-up, then pauses. Switch **Senses to 0** to hold incoming evidence off. Seed a physical event, press Run, and compare the context map against the perceptual readout. Next switch **Coupling to 0**: the same physiological medium remains active but no longer affects interpretation. You can also flip each region's hidden rule and restore sensory evidence to see how the local context responds.

## Why two sheets?

The original [TuftSheet](https://github.com/anttiluode/TuftSheet) showed that a spatial diffusive context could maintain two local hidden rules where one global context performed worse. However, its boundary-learning test failed (22/32 against 24/32 required). Its diffusion is a message-sharing mechanism, not a biological cortical spreading depression wave.

This Sol branch asks a **different, narrower question**. One diffusive field holds internal knowledge; a separate excitable medium can produce travelling disturbances even while the outside world stays unchanged. Coupling the second into the first's **readout** gives an intervention to distinguish three possibilities:

- an external sensory change;
- an actual update to the stored internal world model;
- a disturbance to the physical medium used to read that model.

A persistent context does not imply subjective qualia. And an excitability front that corrupts an output is not evidence that this toy explains scintillating scotoma.

## Model and controls

Context update: a branch compares its local ±1 input against a context-gated expectation, sends the signed residual into the context sheet and propagates that field to neighbours with slow leak. The weak link at the exact left/right midpoint is **given**, not learned.

Excitability update: a generic two-variable reaction–diffusion system, fast activator *u* and slow recovery *v*, on a two-dimensional grid. Its simulation time and lengths are uncalibrated.

Readout: local tanh(context) multiplied by a physiological suppression factor, with an added positive spontaneous-output component. These couplings are **designed assumptions**.

The source contains a paired 32-seed comparison, with identical pre-disturbance training across **seeded coupled**, **seeded uncoupled**, and **unseeded sham** conditions. A 650-tick training phase is followed by a sensory blackout, so the stored model isn't continually retrained during the physical perturbation. The six gates are documented in [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md).

## Running the checks

Use Node.js 18 or newer, without npm install:

    node --test tests/model.test.js
    node experiments/confirm.js
    node experiments/confirm.js results.json

The seed-17 prototype was exploratory development. Any held-seed results must be described as **toy-model confirmation**, not as experimental neuroscience or evidence for human perceptual experience.

## What is still missing

- Learned boundaries instead of a pre-supplied region split.
- A persistent **object** and physical scene, instead of two symbolic hidden rules.
- Multiple modalities and a trained acoustic-to-visual common-cause inference.
- Action-conditioned counterfactuals, not just changing a sign rule.
- Anatomically grounded cortical, ionic and metabolic dynamics, plus retinotopic mapping.
- A readout failure that emerges without the hand-written impairment rule.

**The most important next experiment:** vary the context sheet's persistence mechanism independently of the physiological sheet and measure whether better long-gap prediction genuinely creates an instability tradeoff. At the moment that connection is a hypothesis, not a result.
